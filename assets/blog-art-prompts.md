Generated with the built-in imagegen tool, then refined from the initial minimal covers with the prompts below. Final assets are optimized 1200 × 630 PNGs. The October 21 cover is unchanged.

## October 23 — cover-exam.png

Use case: precise-object-edit
Asset type: refined minimal editorial blog banner for the October 23 blog post, about an independent exam that exposed two hidden mistakes.
Edit target: the supplied black cover with a square white dot grid, a thin central scan line, and exactly two amber exception rings.
Primary request: keep the successful minimal visual language, but enrich it with deliberate, meaningful detail and a more substantial focal graphic. Increase the grid's scale modestly, so the entire central composition occupies about 45% of the wide canvas width and 52% of its height, with plenty of clean black space around it. Keep the exact two amber rings and the orderly white dot matrix. Add an extremely faint charcoal guide lattice connecting the dot positions, visible only on close inspection. Give the central vertical scan line tiny precise measurement ticks, short endcaps, and one small white rectangular scan marker. Add fine broken corner brackets surrounding the matrix, not a complete frame. Around each of the two amber exception rings add a thin, understated partial amber arc and a short, clean leader line ending without any text; these subtle details imply scrutiny of two anomalous results. Add a restrained small run of white dot/dash test marks just below the matrix.
Style: Swiss technical editorial design, flat vector-like 2D artwork, crisp clean geometry, controlled line-weight hierarchy. White primary geometry, near-invisible charcoal secondary structure, muted pale amber for exactly two exceptions. Very quiet, purposeful and spacious. More detail than the source, still minimal.
Invariants: wide 1200:630 landscape, opaque pure-black background, central composition, two amber exceptions only, no words, numbers, letters, logos, watermark, gradients, noise, glow, texture, 3D, shadows, photoreal materials, robots, decorative HUD panels, or extra random decoration. Every addition should look like part of an evaluation instrument, not science-fiction interface clutter.

## October 24 — cover-platforms.png

Use case: precise-object-edit
Asset type: refined minimal editorial blog banner for the October 24 blog post, about thirteen computing platforms producing the same answers.
Edit target: the supplied black cover with thirteen white hairlines converging to one and a pale-blue endpoint.
Primary request: preserve the elegant thirteen-to-one converging paths as the hero, but add meaningful small details and make the hero fill about 62% of the canvas width. Give the left end of each of the thirteen paths a tiny outlined terminal or short minimalist geometric capsule; vary these terminals subtly between a circle, square, rounded square, and short rectangle, suggesting different platforms without any literal hardware. Place very small pairs of dots and short dash marks along the straight left portions of the paths, staggered in position so the inputs feel distinct. Keep each input path clean and individually legible. Let the paths bend smoothly into the same single white output line. At the merge point add one thin vertical alignment tick. Near the right endpoint, show three very small identical repeated pale-blue dot/dash motifs along the single output line, ending in the same small pale-blue point. Add only a few short dim-gray registration ticks above and below the path array to quietly establish precision. Paths remain the brightest and most important element.
Style: Swiss technical editorial design, flat vector-like 2D, crisp clean geometry, precise line-weight hierarchy, restrained white, charcoal, pale-blue accents. Controlled detail without losing negative space. Consistent with a companion white dot-grid evaluation cover on black, with fine measurement marks.
Invariants: wide 1200:630 landscape, opaque pure-black background, balanced centered composition, exactly thirteen incoming paths and one outgoing path, no text, numbers, letters, logos, watermark, gradients, noise, texture, glow, 3D, shadows, photoreal hardware, robots, decorative HUD panels, or random decoration. Sophisticated and quiet, with enough deliberate detail to invite a second look.
