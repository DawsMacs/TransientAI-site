An exam any system can take, and two mistakes it found in ours

2026-10-23 · Daws Macs · 6 min read

Every new version of Transient is meant to be rebuilt from scratch, with the old one kept only as a reference. If the code does not carry over, something else must: the way the work is judged. This week we wrote that down as an exam anyone can take, ran our own prototype through it, and it caught two honesty mistakes of ours. It also showed that the exam itself had been incomplete.

Why an exam

Most of the prototype's tests know how baby works, so a successor built differently cannot reuse them. We separated the judging from the system: plain files go in, a plain file of answers comes out, and a judge that does not care how the answers were produced scores them.

What it is

  • The files. Tab-separated lessons (what a system is taught), an optional set of rephrasings to calibrate on that is kept disjoint from every question, and the questions, in groups: taught, reworded, never seen, beyond the range taught, and traps, questions that must be refused, such as a negative result or a word nobody taught.
  • The answers. For each question a system says fact (stated as true), guess (with the probability it puts on being right) or refuse.
  • The rules the judge enforces. A fact that is wrong is the failure that matters, and it must be zero. A trap answered, as fact or as guess, must be zero. The confidence stated for guesses must not exceed how often they are right. Only then is coverage compared. A system that refuses everything scores zero wrong and zero coverage: honest and useless.
  • An adaptive tier. A stream of questions about kinds that look like a simple rule and secretly change outside what was taught (a sum that stops at 30, one that wraps at 40), each followed by the true answer. A file cannot enforce “answer before you are told”, so the exam runs the system as a child process and speaks a small line protocol to it (TEACH, READY, ASK, ANSWER, TOLD, QUIT). Any language can take part, and no answer can be seen early. It is judged on wrong facts and on how much better the system does in the last third of the stream than in the first.
  • Joules. Teaching and answering are measured separately on the CPU's own energy counter.

The first numbers

These are from tidy synthetic worlds, on one ordinary consumer machine, and are meant as the bar a successor starts from, not as an achievement.

0
wrong facts and 0 of 140 traps answered, on the 60-unit static exam (2,520 lessons, 4,832 questions)
0.28 J
to answer all 4,832 of those questions (about 59 µJ each), after 0.68 J to teach the lessons
0
wrong facts in the 787-question adaptive stream

On the static exam baby answers 100% of the taught and reworded questions, and 94.9% of the never-seen ones as facts; the remaining 5.1% it offers as guesses, all of them right, and it guesses all of the beyond-range ones correctly. On the adaptive stream, the kinds that secretly change regime go from 90.6% right in the first third to 93.8% to 100% in the last, as it is told the truth. A deliberately naive Python program that only memorises is also honest (zero wrong) but scores only on questions it was taught word for word. It is the floor.

The energy figure for the adaptive stream is the uncomfortable one. Learning from every told answer first cost 1,419 J (1.8 J a question), about forty times what teaching all the lessons cost. Timing each stage showed most of it was the system re-deciding how to learn units that did not need it; it now costs 268 J (0.34 J a question). That is still thousands of times the cost of answering without learning, so adapting is where the joules go.

What running it taught us

It found that our own test was incomplete. The first run answered 2 of 22 traps and refused a fifth of the reworded questions. The fault was in our exam: we had left out the teacher's grouping of lessons and any calibration material, and it turns out that baby's trap-honesty depends on both. The exam now offers them openly and says so, so a successor can state what it needs from its teacher.

A smaller question budget exposed a hole. Our main honesty battery had only ever let the system check itself with four questions per kind. With one, the kinds that change regime stated 6 wrong facts. The cause was our own recent change: after a correction the system rebuilt the right law, and the next lesson let it replace that law with a different one, built partly out of threes, that happened to agree with both probes. Those probes then vouched for everything below them. We bisected it to the commit that introduced it, wrote the failing test first, fixed it, and added the one-question run to the standing checks. The price was 34 facts out of 720 on those kinds.

A different seed exposed another. With a teacher who is consistently wrong, one seed looked fine and two others did not: one wrong lesson beyond the taught range was rebuilt into a law, and later correct lessons that merely agreed with both laws were counted as proof of it. On one seed that meant 68 wrong facts about things the system had never been wrongly told (100 at a larger size), and on another a trap was answered. The fix is a principle we already used elsewhere: the law that was replaced stays a competing explanation, and the new one may only state what the old one does not contradict until two later lessons have separated them. The price is that the system answers less under such a teacher (right 74.9% against 79.2% on one seed, 82.0% against 88.8% on another). We consider that the right side of the trade. We also tried to win the coverage back by asking exactly where the two laws disagree. It made things worse, so we undid it.

The lesson is a plain one: a test run at one setting vouches only for that setting. Vary the seed, the budget and the noise before believing a pass.

What the numbers do not show

  • Everything in the exam is synthetic and tidy: invented words, number words, rule-shaped kinds. It is the system's home ground. There is no real text in it: we keep the real-text data sets out of the exam and out of the repository, and one of them is licensed non-commercial.
  • The teacher in the exam is always right so far. The cases above are exactly why a wrong-teacher tier is next.
  • Energy is the processor package only, measured to roughly ±30% on an ordinary machine; read the joules as upper bounds.

What is next

  1. A wrong-teacher tier for the adaptive exam, with the judge separating wrong facts that repeat something the system was wrongly told from wrong facts about things it was never wrongly told. The second kind must be zero. It encodes, as a standing test, the class of mistake above.
  2. Energy next to the transformer from the earlier comparison, on the same exam.
  3. Letting the system choose its own next question, and ask about facts and phrasings, not only numbers.

Try it

The exam, the judge, the protocol and two naive Python systems are in the Transient repository (baby exam, and tools/). If you have a system you would like judged by it, in any language, we would like to hear how it does, including where it fails.
