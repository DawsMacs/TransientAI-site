Progress

Where things stand, and what comes next

Transient started on 2026-10-15. This page has the roadmap first, with a status on each item, then the dated log. It is a summary; the full record with numbers is the repository's experiment log.

Roadmap

Ambitions here are kept in proportion to what has been shown. Items are checkpoints: after each we ask what the result taught, and a result that disappoints tells us where to look next. It does not end the work.

What we do not know yet

These are not boilerplate. Each is something we would want to know before believing the headline numbers.

Real text

Two small word-problem corpora are run privately, and it only guesses on them. Nothing shows behavior on millions of real sentences, or that an architecture like this can reach quality that matters.

Whether "0% wrong" holds

It is a spot check. Every harder test we built found a new way for it to fail, and twice this week a check run at a different setting found wrong facts that the usual one had hidden. We expect more.

Whose language it is

The mechanisms, operations and thresholds are our choices. The system builds and selects inside them. A system that finds mechanisms of a new kind is not yet shown.

Real teachers

The exam's teacher is always right so far. Experiments with a simulated teacher who is wrong show where things break, and we found and fixed one such hole, at a cost in coverage. A person has not yet used the tutor.

Joules

Measured on the CPU's own counter, but processor only, to about ±30%, and only on Linux with hardware that exposes it. Adapting from told answers is still about 600 times the cost of answering without learning.

A teacher who repeats a wrong answer

A source that is consistently wrong about particular facts cannot be caught from inside, and the baby believes it when asked again (14 to 39 repeats in our runs). Damage to things it was never wrongly told is zero so far, which is a spot check.

Other machines

Thirteen CI rows agree on every answer we pin, but they are GitHub's virtual machines and emulation, one image each. Real s390x or riscv64 hardware, other OS versions and the math libraries' last bits across them are not covered, and a latent dependence on hash-map order in two places is known and unfixed.

Data gaps

A lone explanation that fits everything taught can still be wrong at a number nobody taught. Checks and review catch it in our batteries, but nothing structural prevents it.

Timeline

Newest first. Dates are when the work landed; numbers come from the experiment log and carry its caveats.

  1. 2026-10-24

    Thirteen platforms, one set of answers

    CI now builds and tests on Linux (x86_64 glibc and musl, aarch64, 32-bit x86, s390x, riscv64), macOS (Apple silicon and Intel), Windows, FreeBSD, NetBSD, OpenBSD and WebAssembly. A fingerprint and an accuracy audit show the math libraries differ in the last bit, never by more than one step; the exam predictions, the adaptive replies and the scorecard are byte-identical to the Linux reference on every row, and a difference on the reference row now fails the build. DragonFly BSD was tried and dropped (its packaged Rust crashes the program at startup). CI-tested, not a promise of support. The guide to getting and running it is in the docs. Read the post.

  2. 2026-10-23

    Cheaper adapting, and an idea rejected by evidence

    Learning from every told answer fell from 0.22 J to 36 mJ with the replies byte-identical: a settled exact program that still explains every lesson is no longer refitted or re-decided, and a unit is no longer re-decided twice for one answer. A back-off for units that can only memorize looked identical on every clean stream and the whole test battery, but changed two answers on a stream with a wrong teacher, one of them a new wrong fact, so it was dropped. Comparing the answers on that kind of stream is now part of how we accept a cost cut.

  3. 2026-10-23

    The exam, put to a transformer

    The same lessons and judge, a plain attention transformer: it fits what it was taught, then states 1,500 to 2,800 wrong facts and answers 43 to 100% of the traps, depending on whether it refuses below its own confidence (the baby: 0 and 0). Told each truth as a stream goes on, it does not get better (19% right frozen, 11 to 16% fine-tuned); the baby's adapting is the expensive side. One small untuned model, joules on a busy machine.

  4. 2026-10-23

    A wrong teacher in the exam, and the hole it found

    A stream whose truths are consistently wrong about a share of the facts, judged on repeats of a wrongly-told fact apart from damage elsewhere. Its first run found 0, 1 and 1 wrong facts about things the teacher never got wrong: one wrong lesson that a look-alike law agreed with had replaced a correct law. Fixed by treating such a fit as a repair that keeps the old law as a rival; now zero on six runs, pinned and re-checked weekly.

  5. 2026-10-23

    The entry exam, joules, and two mistakes found by changing the test

    A plain-file exam and a pipe protocol that any system can take: no wrong facts, no trap answered, honest confidence, then coverage and joules. The baby: 0 wrong facts and 0 of 140 traps answered, 0.68 J to teach 2,520 lessons and 0.28 J to answer 4,832 questions; on the adaptive stream the rule-changing kinds climb 90.6%, 93.8%, 100%. Running it exposed two honesty holes of ours that a check at one setting had hidden, both fixed. Read the post.

  6. 2026-10-23

    Cost, found by timing each stage

    Teaching the baby on a real-text corpus fell from 111 s to 13.7 s with identical results (a ridge fit that grew with the cube of the vocabulary is now solved from the other side). Learning from every told answer fell from 1,419 J to 268 J for the same stream, once most of it was found to be needless re-deciding.

  7. 2026-10-22

    Real text, a second corpus, and learning from being told

    A learned correction layer lifted the routing of a problem's kind from about 50 to 57% to 70 to 76%; a selector learns which numbers of a story matter from the baby's own programs; a second, differently built corpus (ASDiv) gave the same picture with nothing retuned. On real text it only guesses, and is right a little over half the time, with figures that swing between splits. A person can now answer the baby's own questions (baby tutor).

  8. 2026-10-22

    After a miss, the new regime; and a teacher who is sometimes wrong

    One correction of a sum that caps or wraps now rebuilds the law: all 157 wrong guesses became right guesses. A skeptical baby holds an answer that contradicts a rule it is sure of until it is repeated: with independent slips up to 20% it stays 99.9 to 100% right, against 76.8% for a trusting one. Consistent errors cannot be caught from inside (88% at 20%).

  9. 2026-10-21

    The controlled comparison

    A plain attention transformer written in this repository, and a tuned PyTorch one, against the baby on the same lessons, with energy read from the CPU's own counter. The baby is never wrong on unseen questions where they are wrong 0.1 to 40%, and extrapolates as labeled guesses where they stay at 14 to 28%. The transformers are more accurate on unseen questions from about 60 units, because the baby refuses about 9%. An early claim of ours that its energy advantage shrinks with scale was an artifact of noisy readings and is withdrawn.

  10. 2026-10-21

    The bridge to real text, and the TransientAI name

    A harness that loads real corpora (SVAMP's JSON, or question/answer files), sets aside what the prototype cannot read and says why, teaches a share, and grades the rest, listing every wrong answer. Waiting on a corpus. The project is named Transient; TransientAI is its creator.

  11. 2026-10-21

    Guessing like a scientist

    Careful became the default. Beyond what it was taught or has checked, the baby offers a labeled guess, keeps a record of how often its guesses at each distance are right, and stops guessing where they have been wrong more often than right. A correction ends the guessing in the region it contradicted. Testing this found a hole (a box-shaped reach vouching for an unchecked corner: 4 wrong facts of 9,309) that was fixed at a measured cost in coverage.

  12. 2026-10-21

    A hole found, and a careful mode

    A rule that fits everything taught but changes behavior outside it (a sum that caps or wraps) was answered confidently and wrongly: 53 wrong answers per seed. Careful mode removes them and, after two rounds of the baby's own self-checks, wins back 99.2% of genuine extrapolation with 0 wrong.

  13. 2026-10-21

    Tuning its own bar, adapting its effort

    It measures the share of wrong lessons in its own data (it estimated 9.6% where the world had 9.7%) and sets how reliable a mechanism must be before answering. At 10% label noise, unseen questions answered went from 42% to 98%. Searches that find nothing now fail cheaply (battery 23.5 s to 14 s, same results).

  14. 2026-10-20

    Asking, inventing, and noise

    The baby keeps competing explanations and asks the number that would separate them. It worked out all 16 hidden numeric kinds and 7 of 9 harder ones, with 0 false inventions. With 2% wrong lessons it answers 0% wrong and 97 to 100% of unseen questions right.

  15. 2026-10-20

    Scale

    The world grew to 600 units, 25,776 lessons and a 10,000-word vocabulary. Learning one fact stayed at about half a millisecond; teaching 26k lessons fell from 127 s to 2.65 s after the per-lesson tables were made incremental.

  16. 2026-10-20

    Published, open

    The work went public under the AGPL, with a technical README, CI that fails if the scorecard's WRONG column rises above 0.0%, and a developer certificate of origin instead of a contributor license agreement.

  17. 2026-10-20

    Storage, discovery, light learning, less effort

    The baby found units itself from unlabeled lessons (9 from 792). It can forget what is already explained (49% of the file kept, still answering every lesson). Answering fell from 8,977 operations to double digits, and a rule is fit exactly rather than iterated (392× fewer operations).

  18. 2026-10-20

    One baby

    The separate experiments were unified: 13 units, five mechanisms chosen per unit by evidence, a pipeline that answers with a reason or refuses with one. Scorecard on five seeds: 0% wrong.

  19. 2026-10-19

    Readers, refusal, rules, and a library of ways to learn

    Position-free readers mattered more than any learning trick. The baby learned to say it does not know, fitted a rule for sums from examples (right beyond the range it was taught, where memorizing networks got 5%), and began choosing among mechanisms from held-back evidence.

  20. 2026-10-15

    The first baby

    A 16k-parameter, from-scratch, zero-dependency Rust network that answers a query with one word, plus a joule ledger. A fact store matched retraining's retention at roughly 1/900 of the estimated energy.
