Research / Method

How we judge results, and what we got wrong

A system that bluffs can look excellent on accuracy alone. So we report what matters first: when it was wrong, separately from when it declined to answer.

The rules we hold ourselves to

  1. WRONG is reported apart from refused. A refusal is an acceptable answer; a stated falsehood is not. The CI check fails the build if the scorecard's WRONG column rises above 0.0%.
  2. Never silently wrong. A guess may be wrong, if it is labelled as a guess and its track record is stated. A fact stated and false is the failure that matters most.
  3. Adversarial questions after every new capability. We build the harder test before the feature where we can, and keep batteries of must-refuse questions in the test suite.
  4. Deceptive cases reported apart. Kinds of lesson built to look like one rule and secretly change behaviour are never folded into the headline.
  5. Estimates are labelled as estimates. Energy is counted and priced, not metered, until it is metered.
  6. Mistakes and rejected hypotheses are written down as they happen, in the repository's experiment log.

Standing caveats

  • The lessons are synthetic and tidy: made-up words, small numbers, a handful of phrasings.
  • The mechanisms, the expression language and the thresholds are ours, chosen by hand. The system selects among them; it did not discover them.
  • The experiments use a simulated teacher that is always right.
  • "0% wrong" holds on in-style questions plus our must-refuse batteries. It is a spot check, not a proof.
  • Some constants rest on thin evidence. The 0.8 floor of the reliability bar rests on one 72% failure and six clean seeds.

A list of mistakes

Each of these was found by looking at the cases behind a number, not by reasoning about it. Most of our first explanations were wrong; the experiment log says so.

Dead ends are recorded too. For example, the search budget cannot be cut by watching partial progress, because doomed and deep searches look the same; and an early version of the rule-versus-memory cross-check, which treated a neighbouring lesson as a witness, cut the clean scorecard's never-taught score from 95.1% to 75.6% before the scorecard caught it. The complete record, with the numbers, is the experiment log.
